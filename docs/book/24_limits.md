# Chapter 24 — Limits, absences, and the original request

The first plan contained a section “Not in this build.” This chapter is that section, written as part of the book so it cannot be skipped.

## New mathematical truths (the strong sense)

We store Lean-checked theorems new to this corpus. We do not have a complete literature search, a living open-problem database with formal statements, or a standard for claiming priority. Chapter 18 is the substitute: language discipline.

## A repository of all mathematics

Mathlib is primary. We optionally fetch abstracts and search local folders. We do not ingest encyclopedias in full, OEIS as a formal library, textbooks, or other provers as checkers. Isabelle, Coq, Metamath are hint directories.

## A prover at prover scale

We have a ladder and a beam and a one-attempt explore. We do not have best-first proof search, a Mathlib hammer, trained premise selection, or a CAS-style solver distinct from proving.

## Thousands or millions of live agents

We have a queue. Live work is capped by RAM and cores. Saying “a million agents” without saying “logical” is a lie.

## A research program that invents theories

`research` keeps a definition if a consequence checks. `system propose` can emit axioms. We do not have an automatic theory builder that retains useful notation over weeks.

## Optimal host use

We have CPU, RAM, reserve, priority, Mathlib footprint, disk, optional temperature, optional GPU name, optional TCP latency, and timing steer. We do not have thermal policies beyond 90 C, network-quality routing, or automatic “run Lean on the other box because it is faster.”

## Full distribution

We have a protocol, requeue, token on non-loopback. We do not have TLS, durable queues, authentication beyond a shared string, or a CLI that feeds prove jobs into serve for you.

## Rich inner lives

We have prompt styles and a field wheel. We do not have collaborating agents with memory of each other’s sketches beyond sqlite attempts, and we do not have a daemon.

## A body of work as papers

We have `report.md` and `Report.lean`. We do not have LaTeX papers, journal style, or a human review UI.

## Why list absences

Because a comprehensive book that only celebrates features trains the reader to hallucinate the rest. The software is already useful if you wanted a Lean-checked loop with a host budget and an honest notebook. It is not the completed Leibniz machine. The mapping in Chapter 4 still stands.
